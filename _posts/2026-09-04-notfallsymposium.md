## Evidence synthesis for prognosis of patients witch acute chest pain

Acute chest pain is one of the most common, but also one of the most challenging symptoms in emergency medicine. In most patients, a non-life threatening cause, e.g. musculoskeletal, is found, but in around 6%, moree severe diagnoses, such as acute myocardial infarction, are the cause of symptoms. To assess the risk of individual patients to develop such dangerous diagnoses, scores are frequently being used. One of the most popular ones is the HEART score, developed to predict the risk of major adverse cardiac events within 6 weeks after presentation. It is recommended by international guidelines, and used in EDs throughout the world. Still, its prognostic accuracy has not yet been correctly and systematically evaluated. 

Over the last years, members of our team (Bettina, Karina, Maxi N. and Dominik), together with other  co-authors performed a state-of-the-art systematic review and prognostic meta-analysis, pooling data from 424,701 patients from 106 reports from all over the world. We found that the **discrimination** of the score (*i.e. how well it can differentiate between patients who will suffer an adverse 
event, and those who won't*) is rather good (resulting in a pooled c-statistic of 0.84, with 1.0 being perfect and 0.5 representing pure chance), but the **calibration** of the score (*i.e. how accurate is an individual predicted risk of e.g. 10%*) is almost never reported in studies. We also found that although performance differed significantly between studies, overall they were very similar in different regions of the world as well as countries with different economic status.
